🏠Property Types

apartment / flat

A1

A home on one floor of a larger building. “Flat” is British English.

“This apartment is on the fifth floor.”

house

A1

A building for one family to live in.

“The house has a small garden.”

villa

A1

A large house, often with a garden or pool.

“The villa has a private pool.”

studio

A1

A small apartment with one main room.

“The studio is perfect for one person.”

penthouse

A2

An apartment on the top floor of a building, often luxurious.

“The penthouse has a large roof terrace.”

townhouse

A2

A house joined to other houses in a row, usually on two or three floors.

“This townhouse is close to the city centre.”

off-plan property

B2

A property sold before it is built or finished.

“Off-plan properties are often cheaper than completed ones.”

🛋️Rooms and Spaces

bedroom

A1

A room for sleeping.

“There are three bedrooms upstairs.”

bathroom

A1

A room with a toilet, a sink and a shower or bath.

“The bathroom was renovated last year.”

kitchen

A1

A room for cooking.

“The kitchen is fully equipped.”

living room

A1

The main room for relaxing. Also called a lounge.

“The living room opens onto the balcony.”

balcony

A1

A small outdoor area on the outside wall of a building.

“You can see the sea from the balcony.”

en-suite bathroom

B1

A bathroom connected directly to a bedroom.

“The master bedroom has an en-suite bathroom.”

walk-in closet

B1

A small room for storing clothes. Also called a walk-in wardrobe.

“There’s a walk-in closet next to the bedroom.”

utility room

B1

A room for the washing machine and household items.

“The utility room is next to the kitchen.”

✨Features and Condition

bright

A1

Full of natural light.

“The apartment is very bright in the morning.”

furnished

A2

Sold or rented with furniture.

“The flat is fully furnished.”

sea view

A2

A view of the sea from the property.

“All bedrooms have a sea view.”

spacious

B1

Large, with a lot of space.

“The living area is spacious and comfortable.”

open-plan

B1

Without walls between the kitchen, dining and living areas.

“The apartment features an open-plan living area.”

newly renovated

B1

Recently repaired and improved.

“The house is newly renovated, so you can move in straight away.”

needs updating

B2

In good condition but old-fashioned; it needs some work.

“The kitchen needs updating, which is reflected in the price.”

turnkey

B2

Completely ready to move into.

“It’s a turnkey property — just bring your suitcase.”

📍Location and Area

city centre

A1

The main central part of a city. US English: downtown.

“The apartment is in the city centre.”

near the beach

A1

Close to the beach.

“The villa is near the beach.”

quiet neighbourhood

A2

A calm area where people live.

“It’s a quiet neighbourhood, perfect for families.”

within walking distance

B1

Close enough to walk to.

“Shops and cafés are within walking distance.”

amenities

B1

Useful places and services nearby, such as shops, schools and gyms.

“The area has excellent amenities.”

up-and-coming area

C1

An area that is becoming more popular and valuable.

“It’s an up-and-coming area, so prices are likely to rise.”

💶Money and Numbers

price

A1

How much a property costs.

“The price is 300,000 euros.”

square metres

A2

A unit for measuring area (m²). US English often uses square feet.

“The apartment is 85 square metres.”

deposit

A2

Money paid in advance to reserve a property or before renting.

“The deposit is one month’s rent.”

asking price

B1

The price the seller wants for the property.

“The asking price is negotiable.”

service charge

B1

A regular fee for maintaining the building and shared areas.

“The annual service charge includes the pool and security.”

valuation

B2

A professional estimate of how much a property is worth.

“The bank will arrange a valuation before approving the mortgage.”

rental yield

C1

Yearly rental income as a percentage of the property price.

“This apartment offers a rental yield of around 7%.”
