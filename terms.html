Real Estate Terms A–Z

Professional terminology explained in simple English, with examples you can use with clients. Notes show British and American differences.

ACDEFLMOPRSTV
A
Asking price B1
The price the seller wants for a property. The final price may be lower after negotiation.

“The asking price is €420,000, but the seller is open to offers.”

C
Closing costs B2
Fees paid when a purchase is completed, in addition to the price: taxes, legal fees, registration and bank fees. Mainly US English.

“Closing costs are usually a few percent of the purchase price.”

Commission B1
The fee paid to a real estate agent, often a percentage of the sale price.

“Our commission is 2% of the sale price.”

Completion B2
The final stage of a purchase, when the money is paid and ownership passes to the buyer. In US English: closing.

“Completion is expected in March.”

Conveyancing C1
The legal work of transferring ownership of a property from the seller to the buyer. Mainly British English.

“Your lawyer will handle the conveyancing.”

D
Deposit A2
Money paid in advance to reserve a property or to secure a rental.

“A 10% deposit is required to reserve the apartment.”

Down payment B2
The part of the price the buyer pays upfront; the rest is usually covered by a mortgage.

“The bank requires a down payment of at least 30% for non-residents.”

Due diligence C1
Careful checks before buying: legal status, debts, permits and the condition of the property.

“We recommend completing due diligence before signing the contract.”

E
Escrow C1
An arrangement where a neutral third party holds money or documents until all conditions of the deal are met.

“In off-plan projects, payments often go into an escrow account.”

F
Freehold B2
Ownership of the property and the land it stands on, with no time limit.

“This villa is freehold, so you own the land as well.”

L
Leasehold B2
Ownership of a property for a fixed number of years under a lease; the land belongs to someone else.

“The apartment is leasehold with 99 years remaining.”

Listing B1
A property advertised for sale or rent, or the agreement that allows an agent to sell it.

“The new listing has already had ten enquiries.”

M
Mortgage B1
A loan from a bank to buy a property. The property is security for the loan.

“Are you planning to buy with a mortgage or in cash?”

O
Off-plan B2
Bought before construction is finished, based on plans and a show unit.

“Off-plan buyers usually pay in instalments during construction.”

P
Power of attorney C1
A legal document that lets someone act on your behalf, often used by buyers who live abroad.

“You can sign by power of attorney if you can’t travel.”

R
Rental yield C1
Yearly rental income as a percentage of the property price.

“A gross rental yield of 6% is typical for this area.”

S
Service charge B1
A regular fee for maintaining the building and shared areas. In the US, similar fees are often called HOA fees.

“The service charge covers cleaning, security and the pool.”

T
Title deed B2
The official document that proves who owns a property.

“The title deed will be issued in your name after completion.”

V
Valuation B2
A professional estimate of how much a property is worth.

“The valuation came in slightly above the asking price.”

Viewing A2
A visit to see a property. In US English: showing.

“I can arrange a viewing for Saturday morning.”
